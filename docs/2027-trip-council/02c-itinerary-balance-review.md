# Itinerary Balance Review

_Ceremony date: August 23, 2026. This review tests itinerary shape, not bookability. All 2027 schedules, prices, operations, entry requirements, and weather expectations remain provisional._

## Review Standard

An outline should use no more than 2–3 substantial bases, average 1–2 meaningful experiences on most destination days, preserve unstructured time, and avoid unnecessary flights or hotel changes. Transfer burden, comfort, fatigue, anniversary distinction, and likely value must be credible before presentation.

## 1. Northern Italy + Croatia

**Balance finding:** The three-base, 4/3/4-night structure passes the base and hotel-change limits. Most days contain one principal experience plus lighter time, and Days 4 and 11 provide genuine breathing room. The early Lake Como anniversary day is clear and appropriately protected. There is no avoidable intra-trip flight.

**Reconciliation:** Rai supports the overall pacing but correctly notes that ferries, cobbles, bridges, luggage handling, and the Venice–Rovinj move may be more demanding than “slow” language suggests. The Fact Checker’s stronger objection is controlling: the cross-border transfer and Rovinj departure are load-bearing, and current seasonal service is not a 2027 guarantee. The outline also needs slightly clearer experiential contrast so three Lake Como days and the final Adriatic pause do not read as filler. Value cannot be claimed until costly lodging and transfer assumptions are compared.

**Minimum revisions required:**

1. Set a transfer guardrail before retaining Rovinj: each cross-border or airport movement should have a confirmed primary route, a workable fallback, no more than one connection, and an agreed door-to-door time ceiling.
2. Name the intended departure gateway and show that Day 12 does not require an extra hotel night or a punishing pre-dawn/long-distance transfer.
3. Distinguish the lighter days without adding activity: identify Day 4 as protected recovery with one optional lake experience, and give Day 11 one weather-flexible coastal, cultural, or food anchor.
4. Add equal-status low-walking, non-swimming, and alcohol-free choices; require step/elevator, luggage, and ferry-access checks before booking.

**Status: `outline-ready after revisions`.**

## 2. New Zealand + Australia

**Balance finding:** Two bases and one intra-trip flight are disciplined for a two-country concept, and the daily outline usually stays within 1–2 experiences. Nevertheless, only nine days are full destination days; Pacific transit, jet lag, the Day 8 flight, and another long excursion create the highest fatigue burden. The anniversary anchor exists, but the trip’s value and distinctiveness depend too heavily on an unnamed, weather-sensitive alpine day.

**Reconciliation:** Rai is right that the two-base shape is the least harmful version of this pairing and that the first 48 hours and Day 9 must remain reducible. The Fact Checker is also right that good pacing inside each base does not cure the travel-to-experience ratio, weak historical fit, or lack of weather resilience. A Queenstown-based Milford Sound road day would be especially inconsistent with the comfort goal. Accessibility, alcohol-free, shorter-walk, culturally appropriate, and weather-independent options remain necessary but would not resolve the structural problem.

**Minimum revisions required:**

5. Replace the two-country outline with a 13–14-day New Zealand-only concept using no more than three substantial bases; use a Te Anau stay if Milford Sound is retained rather than treating it as a Queenstown road day.
6. Preserve the first 48 hours as low-stakes recovery, build a movable weather reserve around the principal scenic anchor, and provide shorter/seated alternatives for active days.
7. Reassess anniversary distinction, historical interest, total flight time, and estimated all-in cost before the rebuilt outline returns to review.

**Status: `not ready`.** The current two-country itinerary needs a structural rebuild, not wording-level repair.

## 3. Portugal

**Balance finding:** The two five-night bases, single rail relocation, and lack of intra-trip flights give this outline the best comfort and fatigue profile. Most days have one main experience and room for meals or wandering. The Douro anniversary day is well placed, but several days remain generic, and four out-and-back excursions could quietly replace hotel churn with repeated transfer fatigue.

**Reconciliation:** Rai confirms that “comfortable” does not mean effortless: hills, cobbles, queues, and standing require deliberate transport and rest choices. The Fact Checker correctly identifies insufficient anniversary definition and weather/value assumptions, but adding a third Douro hotel is not yet the minimum balance repair; it would introduce another move before the two-base version has been concretely tested. The preferred first repair is to make the existing outline specific, selective, and weather-flexible.

**Minimum revisions required:**

8. Limit Sintra to one major timed-admission site plus one lighter town, garden, or scenic element; do not combine marquee palaces.
9. Define one Lisbon coast-or-countryside choice and one Guimarães-or-Braga choice, with realistic door-to-door estimates; drop either excursion if the transfer burden crowds out rest.
10. Specify the Douro anniversary anchor, its rain/cancellation fallback, and an equal-status food/history/scenic option without wine or a boat.
11. Protect Day 10 as a true flex or recovery day rather than a fourth required excursion, and add hill-reducing transit, seated-break, and accessible-route options throughout.
12. Test Porto open-jaw departure practicality and compare like-for-like provisional costs including well-located hotels, transfers, timed admissions, the anniversary meal, and major excursions.

**Status: `outline-ready after revisions`.**
