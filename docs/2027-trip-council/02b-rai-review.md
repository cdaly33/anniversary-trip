# RAI Review of Current Shortlist

_Review scope: practical safety, accessibility, fatigue, privacy, responsible travel, and traveler choice. No current country-risk level is asserted here._

## 1. Northern Italy + Croatia — 🟡 Yellow

The outline is workable and thoughtfully paced, but several details should remain choices rather than assumptions.

- **WHAT:** Lake villages, Venice, Rovinj, ferries, and historic centers may involve steps, cobbles, bridges, uneven surfaces, standing, and luggage handling.  
  **WHY:** “Gentle” or “slow” does not necessarily mean accessible or low-effort.  
  **HOW:** Before booking, confirm step-free rooms, elevator access, walking distances, accessible ferry/transfer arrangements, and door-to-door luggage support. Offer shorter routes and water/road alternatives without requiring either traveler to disclose a diagnosis.

- **WHAT:** The Venice-to-Rovinj cross-border transfer and final regional departure are unresolved.  
  **WHY:** A long or multi-connection transfer could undermine the intended recovery time.  
  **HOW:** Set a maximum acceptable door-to-door duration and number of changes; prefer a reserved direct transfer where practical and keep the following evening light.

- **WHAT:** Swimming and wine are presented as default options, while Venice and Lake Como are high-demand destinations.  
  **WHY:** Either traveler may not swim or drink, and concentrated tourism can strain residents and sensitive places.  
  **HOW:** Provide equal-status non-swimming and alcohol-free alternatives. Use timed entry, locally based licensed guides, smaller groups, and less congested hours; follow local visitor rules and avoid short-term lodging that is not clearly compliant.

- **WHAT:** Safety, entry, transport, insurance, and seasonal-operation assumptions remain provisional.  
  **WHY:** Conditions can change before September 2027.  
  **HOW:** Recheck official government travel advice, border/entry rules, weather and ferry notices, insurance coverage, and operator credentials at booking and shortly before departure.

## 2. New Zealand + Australia — 🟡 Yellow

The two-base design is the strongest responsible choice for this long-haul concept, but fatigue remains material.

- **WHAT:** The itinerary counts 14 calendar days but only 11 hotel nights, with major time-zone change and another flight on Day 8.  
  **WHY:** Jet lag can affect judgment, balance, sleep, and enjoyment beyond the first recovery day.  
  **HOW:** Keep the first 48 hours low-stakes, avoid prepaid strenuous activities immediately after arrival, and make Day 9 easily reducible. Confirm that both travelers consider the travel-to-experience ratio worthwhile.

- **WHAT:** “Alpine,” “active,” coastal-walk, beach, wine, and Blue Mountains days may imply hiking ability, comfort with heights, swimming, or alcohol use.  
  **WHY:** Those preferences and capacities have not been established.  
  **HOW:** Present seated/scenic, shorter-walk, alcohol-free, and weather-independent equivalents as normal choices. Verify terrain, toilets, shade/shelter, transport access, and cancellation terms before purchase.

- **WHAT:** Scenic areas can be environmentally sensitive, and cultural experiences may involve Māori or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander heritage.  
  **WHY:** Poorly chosen operators can increase ecological pressure or present living cultures superficially.  
  **HOW:** Favor accredited low-impact operators, small groups, established tracks, wildlife-distance rules, and experiences led or endorsed by the relevant community.

- **WHAT:** Long-haul safety, entry, biosecurity, insurance, weather, and excursion operations may change.  
  **WHY:** The draft cannot establish 2027 conditions.  
  **HOW:** Recheck authoritative government, border/biosecurity, weather, park, and operator sources at booking and pre-departure; ensure insurance addresses disruption and planned activity types.

## 3. Portugal — 🟡 Yellow

This is the lowest-friction outline, but “comfortable” should not be equated with easy walking.

- **WHAT:** Lisbon, Porto, Sintra, and some riverfront or historic areas can require hills, stairs, cobbles, and prolonged standing.  
  **WHY:** Full historic-district days may be tiring even with only one principal activity.  
  **HOW:** Confirm hotel gradients and elevator access, use transit or point-to-point rides strategically, schedule seated breaks, and keep shorter accessible routes available.

- **WHAT:** Sintra, central Lisbon/Porto, and popular Douro experiences may concentrate visitors and create queueing.  
  **WHY:** Crowds increase fatigue and can worsen pressure on residents and heritage sites.  
  **HOW:** Reserve timed entries, visit at less congested hours, avoid combining major Sintra sites, use small-group or public-transport options where suitable, and spend with locally owned businesses beyond the busiest corridors.

- **WHAT:** Wine, river outings, and extensive walking are treated as likely activities.  
  **WHY:** Either traveler may prefer not to drink, use boats, or walk long distances.  
  **HOW:** Give food-, history-, garden-, and scenic-drive alternatives equal prominence; ask about activity preferences rather than inferring limitations or enthusiasm.

- **WHAT:** Rail, entry, weather, insurance, and attraction assumptions are provisional.  
  **WHY:** Availability and requirements may change before late 2027.  
  **HOW:** Recheck official advice, entry rules, transport accessibility, weather, insurance, and attraction operations at booking and shortly before travel.

## Cross-itinerary guardrail

Collect only information needed to arrange the trip. Share dietary, accessibility, identity-document, or celebration details only with providers that need them; use secure booking channels and avoid placing sensitive traveler information in shared itinerary documents.
